In one sentence

Aprumo is a notebook, not a bank. It does not connect to your accounts, does not move money and does not give investment advice: it records what you type and does the arithmetic on top of it.

There is a monthly plan and a yearly one. The price for your region is shown by the App Store before you buy, and the subscription renews on its own until you cancel. If you cancel, the app still opens so you can read and export what is already there.

What Aprumo does, and what it does not

Aprumo records entries, accounts, cards, budgets and goals that you type, and works out balances, forecasts and reports from them.

The limits are worth stating plainly, because this is where the misunderstanding lives:

  • It does not connect to banks. There is no open banking, no automatic statement import and no account reading. Everything comes in by your hand.
  • It does not move money. Marking a bill as paid inside the app pays nothing in the real world.
  • It is not financial advice. The numbers, charts and forecasts are arithmetic on what you entered. Deciding to spend, save or invest is yours, and for professional guidance seek someone qualified.
  • It depends on what you type. Wrong data in is wrong result out, and the app has no way of telling the difference.

What happens if you cancel

Nothing is erased, and the app does not close its doors on you.

Once the subscription ends, Aprumo still opens normally: you can read all of your entries, move between months, see the reports and export the whole spreadsheet. What stops working is recording new things — adding, editing, deleting, settling.

That is deliberate. Your financial history is yours, and taking away access to it as a form of pressure would be indefensible.

If you subscribe again, everything works from exactly where it stopped.
